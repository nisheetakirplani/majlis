The eight changes

Before on the left, after on the right.

1

Make the numbers stand out

Start here
Before

Median quota attainment in Mid-market fell from 92% to 71%.

Q3 is 0.4 points above Q2.

After

Mid-market quota attainment 71% (from 92%).

Attrition 3.1% (was 2.7%).

Why: the eye finds the figure first, and change is always written one way, so there is nothing to decode. Figures use equal-width digits so they line up. Risk: too much bold stops working; one figure per sentence.

2

Order findings as a story

Start here
Before
  • 31 left Sales…
  • Mid-market is where…
  • The rise began…
  • 12 of 22…
  • Attainment fell…
After

What happened

  • 31 left Sales…

Where

  • Mid-market…

Why

  • Commission change…

Why: five equal bullets hide the chain of cause; three short labels make the reasoning readable at a glance. Risk: not every question has a "why"; use only the labels that apply.

3

Lighter source marks

Start here
Before

31 people left Sales in Q3. HRMS

After

31 people left Sales in Q3.1

¹ opens the same row; listed once in Sources.

Why: one small number per claim reads like a report and removes the coloured blocks, while checking stays one tap away. Risk: numbers say less than names; hovering shows the system and record.

4

Mark how sure each finding is

Before

The rise began when the commission plan changed.

Certainty only in the confidence line above.

After

The rise began when the commission plan changed. Likely

12 of 22 named it. Partial

Why: executives quote single sentences; each one should carry its own certainty. Confirmed facts stay unmarked. Risk: too many marks look unsure; only "Likely" and "Partial".

5

Gaps as short, specific lines

After

Why: the kind of gap comes first in three words, so the list scans like a checklist and says which figure was used. Risk: labels must stay consistent across answers.
